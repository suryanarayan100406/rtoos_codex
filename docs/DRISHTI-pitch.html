The gap

Multi-pass mapping can't answer a single-pass mission.

Photogrammetry suites — Pix4D, Metashape, DJI Terra, RealityCapture — are accurate, but they assume a planned survey: 70–80% image overlap flown as a grid, ground control points laid on the site, and hours of offline Structure-from-Motion afterwards. In rapid reconnaissance, disaster response, or a contested corridor, there is one pass and one chance — often with no time or safety margin to place control or re-fly.

Assumption 1

A dense grid

Overlapping multi-angle passes the single mission never gets to fly.

Assumption 2

Ground control

Surveyed targets on the ground for metric accuracy — impractical or unsafe on-site.

Assumption 3

Hours offline

Batch reconstruction after landing — no situational picture while it still matters.

The reframe

A single pass is weak on geometry — but rich in signal.

Short baselines and limited angles starve classical triangulation. But one pass still carries a strong satellite-navigation and temporal signal — and inertial too, when an IMU is aboard — while today's learned priors are strong. DRISHTI is a prior-assisted, sensor-fused, streaming reconstruction system — not a classical photogrammetry clone. Three ideas carry the whole design.

Idea 01

Learned geometry beats missing views

Feed-forward multi-view models and metric monocular depth supply dense geometry exactly where short baselines break triangulation — each output carrying a per-pixel confidence.

Idea 02

Sensors supply the metric truth

A tightly-coupled visual + GNSS factor graph — with IMU, RTK/PPK and baro folded in whenever they're present — fixes scale and georeference from the aircraft, the job ground control points normally do. Only video + GPS are required.

Idea 03

Everything streams, everything degrades

A live coarse map on the edge in flight; a refined metric model on the ground in minutes. Every stage emits confidence and has a fallback.

Design anchors

Four commitments, repeated at every layer.

A1

Prior-assisted geometry

Fuse classical multi-view constraints with learned depth & pointmap priors — never triangulation alone.

A2

Metric spine

A visual + GNSS factor graph — IMU, RTK/PPK and baro added when present — yields a metric, georeferenced trajectory without GCPs. The baseline needs only video + GPS.

A3

Two output paths

A Live path (edge, near-real-time) and a Refine path (ground, minutes). The live map survives even if refine is interrupted.

A4

Reliability spine

Every stage emits confidence and has a fallback; output is always a best-effort model plus an uncertainty report.

Architecture

Two coordinated paths across three tiers.

The Edge tier on the UAV (NVIDIA Jetson Orin) runs the Live path S0–S5 — a near-real-time coarse preview plus a coverage-and-quality check while the flight is still useful. The Ground tier (RTX-class GPU) runs the Refine path S6–S10 for the accurate, textured, georeferenced deliverables. A Cloud tier is optional — never required for a mission, so the system stays air-gap friendly.

Anchor A2 · in detail

Metric, without ground control points.

Only video + GPS + flight metadata are mandatory. Scale comes from GNSS baselines and visual structure — removing monocular scale ambiguity with no control points — and camera intrinsics are self-calibrated when not supplied. An IMU (acceleration against gravity), RTK/PPK and baro are optional enhancers that tighten the solution when present. Georeference comes from GNSS(+RTK/PPK), aligning the local solution to WGS84 and projecting to UTM; robust kernels reject satellite-navigation outliers, and covariance flows all the way into the accuracy report.

Accuracy, stated honestly

The bar is ≤ 1 m. One number would still be a lie — here are the regimes.

The official target is ≤ 1 m absolute spatial accuracy. Accuracy is sensor-configuration-dependent and vertical is the weaker axis, so we report by regime — and flag any region that would exceed 1 m rather than hide it behind an average. These are engineering design targets to be measured on the event dataset — not certified results.

Anchor A4 · the ladder

It degrades. It doesn't die.

"Never fails" is not a slogan — it's an engineered ladder. Each trigger has a defined behaviour and a known product impact, and the system always emits a best-effort model plus an explicit uncertainty and coverage report. Nothing crashes silently; nothing ships as unflagged garbage.

The honest envelope

What we claim — and what we deliberately don't.

Credibility is a feature. We would rather win on a promise we can keep than lose on one we can't. This is the line we hold in every document and every demo.

What DRISHTI claims

  • Near-real-time live preview on the edge, plus a refined model on the ground — under 15 minutes for a 10-minute video (design target).
  • Runs on the mandatory inputs alone — video + GPS + flight metadata — with intrinsics self-calibrated; IMU, RTK/PPK and baro only sharpen it.
  • Metric & georeferenced to a ≤ 1 m target — centimetre-class with RTK/PPK, sub-metre-to-≤ 1 m GPS-only, flagged wherever it would exceed it.
  • Graceful degradation with no single point of failure, and an explicit uncertainty & coverage report.
  • Standard deliverables — OBJ · PLY · LAS · GeoTIFF · glTF/GLB · FBX, DSM/DTM & orthomosaic, in a web or desktop viewer.

What DRISHTI does not claim

  • A full 4K textured mesh in hard real time on the drone — the fidelity comes on the ground.
  • Survey-grade certification or complete 360° building facades from a single pass.
  • Measured geometry for occluded / back-facing surfaces — those are inferred and flagged.
  • A single accuracy figure independent of the sensor configuration flown.
The official scorecard

Six weighted criteria. We built for the heaviest first.

NTRO scores this problem on a fixed rubric. We didn't spread effort evenly — accuracy, completeness and speed are 70% of the score, and they map one-to-one onto the metric spine, the coverage report and the two-path pipeline. Nothing here is bolted on to chase points; each criterion is won by a part of the architecture that already had to exist.

The niche no incumbent occupies

Single-pass → georeferenced, metric, textured 3D — near-real-time, with graceful degradation.

Honestly: for a planned multi-pass mission with control points, the offline incumbents beat us on absolute accuracy, facade completeness and mesh polish. We don't pretend otherwise — we use them as our offline refinement oracle and benchmark ceiling, and we compete where they structurally can't: one pass, right now, on-site.

Built to ship — and to stay sovereign

Adopt, don't invent. The IP is the orchestration.

Every model is a swappable block behind a confidence interface — so the fast-moving research field can be tracked without re-architecting, and any component can be retrained on indigenous data. Licensing is a first-class constraint: several state-of-the-art models are non-commercial or exclude military use, so the shipped default is permissive throughout.

◇

Permissive by default

VGGT's commercial checkpoint excludes military use and MASt3R / UniDepth are non-commercial — so we ship Depth Anything 3, MapAnything, Metric3D v2, gsplat and GLOMAP/COLMAP, keeping the restricted models only as a benchmark reference.

⛨

Fully offline / air-gapped

No mission requires the cloud. Weights, coordinate grids and the whole toolchain run on a field workstation — a data-residency guarantee, not a hope.

✦

NavIC / IRNSS ready

Multi-constellation positioning including India's own navigation satellites, with RTK corrections from a sovereign base — reducing reliance on foreign constellations in contested airspace.

↻

Deterministic & auditable

Store-and-forward recording + pinned, seed-fixed ground reprocess means the same pass yields the same model — every accuracy claim is checkable.
